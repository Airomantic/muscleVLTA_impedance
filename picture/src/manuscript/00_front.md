# muscleVLTA: Learning Joint Force Representations for Causal Action–Visuotactile Modeling

@authors Zongqing Jiang, Zixiang Jiang, Ang Li, Daoping Sun, Jidong Zhang

@abstract

Vision-language-action (VLA) models fail in the last centimeters of contact-rich manipulation: similar motions hide different contact states, and the cameras see only the gripper. Force-aware VLAs read a single force signal, and it is unknown whether a policy trained with intermittent visuotactile signals uses them. We present muscleVLTA, a reactive VLA with a joint force representation. On top of π_{0.5}, a 10 Hz reactive expert reads, through latency-causal attention, a one-second memory of the seven joint torques, through which the arm senses every load on its own body, the end-effector wrench and two contact-gated visuotactile fingers; a training-only forward model predicts the next force and touch readings from this memory and the commands. On occluded charger insertion muscleVLTA succeeds in 55 of 60 real-robot trials from a near start and in 13 of 60 from a far start, where π_{0.5} and GR00T succeed at most once, LIFT in 4 and 2 trials and ForceVLA2 in 60.5% and 5 of 60. A counterfactual sensor attribution shows that the policy follows the level of the arm force, a posture cue, and is almost invariant to touch at test time. Balanced fusion separates the sustained load from the interaction force and normalizes attention per sensor stream, which makes the actions 25–30% less sensitive to that level at the same real-robot success. muscleVLTA-RL then fine-tunes the policy offline on its own rollouts with a critic that reads the same force and touch history. {{+ results of the other tasks and of RL (B1, B10)}} A reactive update takes about 60 ms.
