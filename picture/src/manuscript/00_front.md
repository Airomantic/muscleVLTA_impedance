# muscleVLTA: Learning Joint Force Representations for Causal Action–Visuotactile Modeling

@authors Zongqing Jiang, Zixiang Jiang, Ang Li, Daoping Sun, Jidong Zhang

@abstract

Vision-language-action (VLA) models generalize across scenes and instructions, yet they fail in the last centimeters of contact-rich manipulation, where vision is occluded and success depends on force and touch. When a charger is inserted, the gripper and the plug hide the socket from both the external and the wrist camera. Existing force-aware VLAs mostly condition on a single force signal such as the end-effector wrench, and visuotactile signals are intermittent: they carry information only during contact, so that it is unclear whether a policy trained with them actually uses them. We present muscleVLTA, a reactive VLA that learns a joint force representation linking actions to visuotactile perception. On top of the π_{0.5} prior, a 10 Hz reactive expert reads, through latency-causal attention, a joint memory of joint torques, end-effector wrench and two contact-gated visuotactile fingers over a 1 s history, while a training-only forward model predicts the next force and touch readings from this memory and the commands. On occluded charger insertion with 130 demonstrations, muscleVLTA succeeds in 55 of 60 real-robot trials when starting within 20 cm of the socket and in 13 of 60 when starting 40–50 cm away, where π_{0.5} fails (0/60) and the same model without touch reaches 3/60. {{+ baseline numbers once Table 1 is complete (checklist B1)}} A counterfactual sensor attribution then shows what the policy reads: its actions follow the level of the arm force, a posture cue, and are almost invariant to the tactile input at test time, which suggests that touch helps as supervision of the force representation {{to be confirmed by the test-time ablation (B4)}}. We therefore propose balanced fusion, which separates the sustained load from the interaction force and normalizes attention within each sensor stream; in matched offline pilots it makes the actions 25–30% less sensitive to the force level at equal held-out error. Because prediction happens in force and touch space and only in training, a reactive update takes about 60 ms.
