## 1. Introduction

Robot manipulation is a dynamic interaction between a robot and its environment, not only a change of pose. The motion of an arm results from inertia, gravity, actuator torques and contact forces, so similar kinematic states can be different mechanical states: a plug blocked by the rim of a socket hardly moves while the reaction force rises. Vision-language-action (VLA) models [@pi0; @pi05; @openvla; @gr00tn1] generate actions from images and kinematic states. This suffices in free space and breaks down in the last centimeters of contact: when a robot inserts a charger, plug and gripper cover the socket for both cameras, and force is what remains to tell the contact state.

*Which force?* Sensors at different places observe different parts of the interaction. Under closed-loop control a joint torque is not commanded, it results: the actuator supplies whatever the motion requires. The seven joint torques therefore report every load on the arm, its own inertia, friction and gravity as well as a payload, a dragging cable or a contact anywhere along the chain. They let a robot notice changes of its own body, as a person feels a heavier coat and keeps working. The end-effector wrench is the resultant at the tool, and visuotactile fingertips show the local contact geometry and its change. These observations are mechanically related, and none replaces another; yet force-aware VLAs read the wrench [@forcevla; @forcevla2; @lift] or the joint torques [@tavla], and tactile VLAs add fingertip images [@tactilevla; @vtla; @omnivtla; @tafvla; @rdp; @trex; @twla]. *Is the added sensor used?* A higher success rate of a model trained with a sensor does not show that the policy reads it at test time: touch is silent in more than half of our demonstration frames, a dominant modality can suppress the others [@wang2020; @ogm], and an imitation learner may latch onto a nuisance correlate [@dehaan].

Sensing must also close the loop. Impedance control [@hogan] and hybrid force–position control [@raibert; @mason] adapt the interaction to the measured force during execution, but they do not decide what a disturbance means; that is left to the level above. In a VLA this level generates a chunk of actions once per second, inference takes time, and the sensors are sampled at different instants.

We present muscleVLTA, a reactive VLA built on π_{0.5} that learns a *joint force representation*. As in LIFT [@lift], a reactive expert refreshes the actions of a chunk at 10 Hz from a memory of recent sensor readings, under an explicit latency. Our memory joins the joint torques and the end-effector wrench that the arm itself reports, without an add-on force–torque sensor, with two visuotactile fingers over a one-second history; a contact gate lets a finger speak only while it touches something. A forward model, used in training only, predicts the next force and touch readings from the memory and the commands, which ties every action to the response that follows it.

We then ask what the trained policy reads. A counterfactual sensor attribution, which resamples each action chunk from altered readings, shows that the policy follows the *level* of the arm force, which mostly reflects the posture, and that its actions are almost invariant to touch at test time, although the model trained with touch succeeds far more often than the same model without it. Touch thus appears to act through training, and the level is a liability: a payload or a dragging cable changes it without changing the posture. *Balanced fusion* therefore splits the arm force into a sustained load and the interaction force relative to it and keeps one attention distribution per sensor stream. Finally, imitation does not tell a policy which of its own contacts fail: *muscleVLTA-RL* improves the policy offline from its own rollouts in the manner of π^{*}_{0.6} [@recap], with a critic that reads the same force and touch history.

On occluded charger insertion with 130 demonstrations, muscleVLTA succeeds in 55 of 60 real-robot trials from a start within 20 cm of the socket and in 13 of 60 from 40–50 cm, against 60.5% and 5 of 60 for the best baseline. {{+ other tasks (B1)}} Our contributions are:

• a joint force memory for reactive VLAs that combines joint torques, end-effector wrench and contact-gated visuotactile fingers over a sensor history, read under latency-causal attention;

• an action-conditioned forward model of the next force and touch readings that shapes this memory at no inference cost;

• a counterfactual sensor attribution that separates what a policy reads from what it was trained with, and balanced fusion, which separates the sustained load of the arm from the interaction force;

• muscleVLTA-RL, offline advantage-conditioned fine-tuning with a critic that reads the force and touch history of the policy, and real-robot results on occluded charger insertion against four baselines.
