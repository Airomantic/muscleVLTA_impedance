## 1. Introduction

Vision-language-action (VLA) models [@pi0; @pi05; @openvla; @gr00tn1] carry visual and semantic knowledge across scenes, objects and instructions. They break down in the last centimeters of contact-rich manipulation, where the decisive variables are no longer visible. When a robot inserts a charger, the plug and the gripper cover the socket as soon as the plug is above it: neither an external camera nor a wrist camera shows the holes or the prongs, and what remains is what the robot feels.

Two lines of work bring this feeling into VLAs. Force-aware policies inject the end-effector wrench [@forcevla; @forcevla2; @lift] or the joint torques [@tavla], and tactile policies add fingertip images [@tactilevla; @vtla; @omnivtla; @tafvla], often with a fast pathway that reacts to touch between two visual updates [@rdp; @trex; @twla]. Two questions remain open. *Which force?* The wrench summarises the load transmitted through the tool, joint torques distribute it over the kinematic chain, and fingertip sensors measure it where the object is held; existing VLAs mostly read one of them. *Is the added sensor used?* A higher success rate of a model trained with a sensor does not show that the policy reads that sensor at test time. Touch is silent in more than half of the frames of our demonstrations, a dominant modality is known to suppress the others in multimodal learning [@wang2020; @ogm], and an imitation learner given more inputs may latch onto a nuisance correlate [@dehaan].

We present muscleVLTA, a reactive VLA built on π_{0.5} that learns a *joint force representation*. As in LIFT [@lift], a reactive expert refreshes the actions of a chunk at 10 Hz from a memory of recent sensor readings, under an explicit latency. Our memory joins the seven joint torques and the end-effector wrench that the arm itself reports, without an add-on force–torque sensor, with two visuotactile fingers (fingertip wrenches and indentation depth maps with their temporal change) over a one-second history. A contact gate lets a finger speak only while it touches something. A forward model, used in training only, predicts the next force and touch readings from the memory and the commands, so that force is learned as the link between what the robot commands and what its fingertips feel.

We then ask what the trained policy reads. A counterfactual sensor attribution, which resamples each action chunk from altered readings, gives two answers that success rates cannot give. The policy follows the *level* of the arm force, which mostly reflects the posture and thus replaces the proprioceptive state that the policy does not receive; and its actions are almost invariant to the tactile input at test time, although the model trained with touch succeeds four times as often as the same model without it. Touch therefore appears to act through training. From the first finding we derive *balanced fusion*: the arm force is split into a sustained load and the interaction force relative to it, and the reactive expert keeps one attention distribution per sensor stream.

This view also bears on the debate between VLAs and world action models (WAMs), which predict future video together with actions and are argued to generalise better because they model dynamics [@dreamzero; @gr00t2; @wamstudy]. Prediction need not be visual, and it need not happen at test time: Fast-WAM reports that the benefit of video prediction comes mainly from training [@fastwam], and the GR00T 2 whitepaper lists what to predict, and how to add force and touch as inputs, among its open problems [@gr00t2]. muscleVLTA predicts a few dozen force and touch values instead of images, only during training, in the modality that vision cannot supply under occlusion, and keeps a reactive update of about 60 ms.

On occluded charger insertion with 130 demonstrations, muscleVLTA succeeds in 55 of 60 real-robot trials from a start within 20 cm of the socket and in 13 of 60 from 40–50 cm, where π_{0.5} fails in all 60 trials. {{+ one sentence on the baselines (B1)}} Our contributions are:

• a joint force memory for reactive VLAs that combines joint torques, end-effector wrench and contact-gated visuotactile fingers over a sensor history, read under latency-causal attention;

• an action-conditioned forward model of the next force and touch readings that shapes this memory at no inference cost;

• a counterfactual sensor attribution that separates what a policy reads from what it was trained with, and balanced fusion, which weakens the posture shortcut that the attribution reveals;

• real-robot results on occluded charger insertion, and the attribution and smoothness tools used to analyse them.
