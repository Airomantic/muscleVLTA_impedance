## 2. Related Work

**Vision-language-action policies.** π_{0} [@pi0] and π_{0.5} [@pi05] attach a flow-matching action expert [@flowmatching] to a pre-trained vision-language model and generate action chunks [@act; @diffusionpolicy]; OpenVLA [@openvla], GR00T N1 [@gr00tn1] and π_{0.7} [@pi07] likewise couple a pre-trained backbone with action generation, and real-time chunking [@rtc] keeps consecutive chunks consistent under inference delay. We keep the π_{0.5} backbone unchanged and add what it lacks for contact.

**Force and torque in VLAs.** ForceVLA [@forcevla] fuses the external wrench at the tool with vision-language features through a force-aware mixture of experts, and ForceVLA2 [@forcevla2] extends this to hybrid force–position control, where the policy also outputs force targets. TA-VLA [@tavla] studies joint torques instead: torque adapters work best in the action decoder, the torque history is best summarised in a single token, and predicting future torque as an auxiliary output improves the policy. FoAR [@foar] gates force–vision fusion with a predicted future contact. LIFT [@lift], our starting point, grafts a reactive expert beside the action expert of π_{0.5} and injects the recent end-effector force through a causal memory and zero-initialised cross-attention, so that actions are refreshed within a chunk. muscleVLTA inherits this reactive expert and its latency mask. It differs in what the memory holds (joint torques, wrench and fingertip sensing over a history, instead of the recent end-effector force), in the forward model across these modalities, and in testing what the policy reads.

**Tactile and visuotactile policies.** Lee et al. [@lee2019] learned a joint representation of vision and force with self-supervised prediction, including action-conditional contact. 3D-ViTac [@vitac3d] fuses dense tactile arrays with vision for diffusion policies, and Reactive Diffusion Policy [@rdp] pairs a slow latent diffusion policy with a fast tactile-feedback decoder. Tactile-VLA [@tactilevla], VTLA [@vtla], FuSe [@fuse], OmniVTLA [@omnivtla], VLA-Touch [@vlatouch] and TaF-VLA [@tafvla] bring tactile sensing into VLAs, and T-Rex [@trex] adds a variable-rate mixture of transformers for tactile reactivity. T-WLA [@twla] lets a light tactile editor correct the unexecuted actions and supervises it, in training only, with future-image and future-pressure prediction. Our forward model shares this training-only use of prediction; it is conditioned on the commands, and its targets join the arm force with the fingertip readings. In addition we measure whether touch is read at test time.

**World action models.** WAMs generate future observations together with actions. DreamZero [@dreamzero] builds on a 14B video diffusion model and reaches closed-loop control at 7 Hz; GR00T 2 [@gr00t2] generates future video latents and an action chunk with a single 2B-parameter transformer; Cosmos Policy [@cosmospolicy], UWM [@uwm] and Video Prediction Policy [@vpp] couple video prediction and control in other ways [@wamsurvey]. Whether prediction must run at test time is debated: FLARE [@flare] aligns policy features with future latents without generating frames, and Fast-WAM [@fastwam] finds that skipping future prediction at test time costs little, whereas removing video co-training costs much. Tactile-WAM [@tactilewam] brings touch into a WAM with asymmetric attention, so that tactile tokens inform the actions without disturbing video prediction. We take the training-signal view for contact, without a video model (Section 5).

**Shortcuts, modality imbalance and attribution.** In imitation learning, access to more information can yield worse policies, because the learner may explain the expert's actions by a nuisance correlate [@dehaan; @wen2020]; this is one form of shortcut learning [@geirhos]. In multimodal networks the modalities are learned at different rates and a dominant one can suppress the others [@wang2020; @ogm]; modality dropout is a standard remedy [@moddrop]. Whether attention weights explain what a model uses is contested [@jain; @wiegreffe]. We therefore intervene on the inputs and measure the change of the output, and find such a shortcut in the force input of a VLA.
