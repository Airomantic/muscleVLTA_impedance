## 2. Related Work

**Vision-language-action policies.** π_{0} [@pi0] and π_{0.5} [@pi05] attach a flow-matching action expert [@flowmatching] to a pre-trained vision-language model and generate action chunks [@act; @diffusionpolicy]; OpenVLA [@openvla], GR00T N1 [@gr00tn1] and π_{0.7} [@pi07] likewise couple a pre-trained backbone with action generation, and real-time chunking [@rtc] keeps consecutive chunks consistent. We keep the π_{0.5} backbone and add what it lacks for contact.

**Force and torque: from control to VLAs.** Classical force control regulates the interaction at the level of execution, through a commanded impedance [@hogan] or by controlling force along constrained and position along free directions [@raibert; @mason], and joint torques have long served to detect and isolate external forces along an arm [@haddadin]. In VLAs, ForceVLA [@forcevla] fuses the external wrench at the tool with vision-language features, and ForceVLA2 [@forcevla2] extends this to hybrid force–position control, where the policy also outputs force targets. TA-VLA [@tavla] studies joint torques instead and finds that predicting future torque as an auxiliary output improves the policy. FoAR [@foar] gates force–vision fusion with a predicted future contact. LIFT [@lift], our starting point, grafts a reactive expert beside the action expert of π_{0.5} and injects the recent end-effector force through a causal memory and zero-initialised cross-attention, so that actions are refreshed within a chunk. muscleVLTA inherits this reactive expert and its latency mask. It differs in what the memory holds (joint torques, wrench and fingertip sensing over a history), in the forward model across these modalities, and in testing what the policy reads.

**Tactile and visuotactile policies.** Lee et al. [@lee2019] learned a joint representation of vision and force with self-supervised prediction; 3D-ViTac [@vitac3d] and Reactive Diffusion Policy [@rdp] add tactile feedback to diffusion policies. Tactile-VLA [@tactilevla], VTLA [@vtla], FuSe [@fuse], OmniVTLA [@omnivtla], VLA-Touch [@vlatouch], TaF-VLA [@tafvla] and T-Rex [@trex] bring tactile sensing into VLAs. T-WLA [@twla] lets a tactile editor correct the unexecuted actions and supervises it, in training only, with future-image and future-pressure prediction. Our forward model shares this training-only use of prediction; it is conditioned on the commands, and its targets join the arm force with the fingertip readings.

**World action models.** WAMs generate future observations together with actions [@dreamzero; @gr00t2; @cosmospolicy; @uwm; @vpp; @wamsurvey; @wamstudy]. Whether prediction must run at test time is debated: FLARE [@flare] aligns policy features with future latents without generating frames, Fast-WAM [@fastwam] finds that skipping future prediction at test time costs little whereas removing video co-training costs much, and Tactile-WAM [@tactilewam] brings touch into a WAM. We take the training-signal view for contact, without a video model: the forward model predicts a few dozen force and touch values, in training only.

**Reinforcement learning for VLAs.** π^{*}_{0.6} [@recap] improves a VLA from its own experience: a value function scores the recorded actions, and the policy is trained conditioned on a binarised advantage. With force or touch, TORL-VLA [@torlvla] refines the actions of a wrench-aware VLA by online RL, ForceRFT [@forcerft] learns a residual policy and critics conditioned on the wrist wrench on top of a frozen VLA, and TacCoRL [@taccorl] trains with RL in a real-aligned simulator. muscleVLTA-RL follows the recipe of π^{*}_{0.6}, which needs neither online updates on the robot nor a simulator; it fine-tunes the whole policy, and its critic reads the joint torques, the wrench and the fingertip maps that the policy reads.

**Shortcuts and attribution.** In imitation learning, more information can yield worse policies, because the learner may explain the expert's actions by a nuisance correlate [@dehaan; @wen2020], a form of shortcut learning [@geirhos]; in multimodal networks a dominant modality can suppress the others [@wang2020; @ogm; @moddrop]. Whether attention weights explain what a model uses is contested [@jain; @wiegreffe]. We therefore intervene on the inputs and measure the change of the output, and find such a shortcut in the force input of a VLA.
